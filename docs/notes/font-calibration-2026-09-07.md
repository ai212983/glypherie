calibration fonts at the plan's sizes, weights 400/600, binary and Inkplate gray
output, small-size threshold comparisons, and width/optical-size probes. Focused
follow-ups vary thresholds on the configurations that showed structural defects.

The run records font, configuration, generator source, dependency-lock, and artifact
checksums; Rust toolchain identity; actual raster x-height; and text-run advances
and kerning. Raw output is under `tools/font_probe/out/calibration/` (ignored):
`manifest.json`, `report.md`, `glyphs/`, and magnified `failures/` images. Regenerate
from the checked-in configuration to reproduce these files.

Topology checks cover `bhmnpo08`, using eight-connected ink and four-connected
background. Gray is projected at half coverage for this diagnostic. Other letters,
punctuation, and units are generated and used in specimens/text-run measurements;
they do not all have an automated topology expectation.

## Findings and calibration decisions

User review identified an uneven right stem in Roboto's 16px `m` at threshold 128.
A focused generator comparison confirms lower-stem widths of 2/2/1 pixels with
`auto_fallback` and `interpreter`; explicit `auto` produces 1/1/1. This is a hinting
candidate, with shoulders and cross-glyph consistency still to evaluate. Topology
checks alone missed this defect. Glyph-level consistency review now precedes text
preferences and board evaluation.

The focused `compare_hinting.py` experiment uses the same Roboto asset, 16px,
weight 400 and width 100. FreeType 2.13.2 with `FT_LOAD_TARGET_MONO`,
`FT_LOAD_MONOCHROME`, and `FT_LOAD_RENDER` produces lower `m` stem widths of
2/2/2 pixels. Explicit automatic hinting produces 1/1/1; the current generator path
produces 2/2/1. Enlarged `bhmnpu` and native-size samples are ready for human review
under `tools/font_probe/out/hinting-review/roboto16/`. FreeType mono is a promising
candidate; the comparison changes both hinting implementation and scan conversion,
so causal isolation and wider glyph/size/family checks remain open. Generator
defaults remain unchanged.

The follow-up `outline_experiment.py` sweep tests 35 shared growth/alignment settings
on `bhmnpdq`, holding FreeType mono fixed. All preserve the expected connected ink
and counter counts. Grid shifts alone give no improvement under the sampled-band
diagnostic. Horizontal outline growth of 0.375px, recentered then shifted right by
0.125px, makes all sampled strokes two pixels wide, reducing the band penalty from
12 to 0. The same transform applies to every glyph; advances remain unchanged.

User review accepts the thicker bowls and their outward bumps. The remaining defect
is loss of entry details at the upper-left of `m`, `n`, and `p`, and upper-right of
`q`. Thickness and topology checks need join-detail preservation checks before
they can guide automatic selection. The experiment records full glyphs,
settings, source checksum, tool version, script hash, and per-row measurements under
`tools/font_probe/out/outline-experiment/`.

The follow-up `join_experiment.py` keeps the same growth and shift, while protecting
narrow exterior white gaps between ink runs in the baseline bitmap. The rule uses
geometry with four-connected background, without character IDs or fixed positions.
It restores one pixel at each flagged entry, and analogous lower details in `b` and
`d`. Both tested gap widths (1px and 2px) produce the same output for this group.
All seven glyphs retain expected ink/counter topology and the sampled two-pixel
stroke widths. The comparison includes an unhinted source-outline render at 7× size.

User review confirms restoration of the flagged entries, while identifying remaining
detail loss in `b`, `h`, and `d`. Baseline-derived protection cannot recover details
absent from that baseline; broader repertoire/size/family checks remain open.
Evidence and images are under `tools/font_probe/out/join-experiment/`.

The analysis-only `analyze_features.py` detects inward turns on the combined filled
source boundary. Combining overlaps is necessary: Roboto builds stems and bowls
from overlapping contours whose separate boundaries conceal their visible junctions.
At the recorded 0.4px reach and 45° turn threshold, it finds 12 candidate junctions:
two each in `b`, `m`, `p`, `d`, `q`, and one each in `h`, `n`. The same geometry rule
applies to every glyph, including the upper joins of `b`, `h`, and `d`.

Orange boxes mark these candidates on source outlines, baseline bitmaps, and the
current constrained output. Local source-to-bitmap extra/missing-ink measurements
are identical between the two bitmaps at these regions. They include displacement
from hinting and are not proof of feature loss. Detector coverage and correspondence
to the user's observations await review; semantic stem/bowl classification and
feature correspondence after hinting remain open. Four synthetic tests cover
convex shapes, reflection/translation, overlap union, and counter preservation.
Artifacts are under `tools/font_probe/out/feature-analysis/`; rendering is unchanged.

User review confirms the upper detection regions for `b`, `h`, and `d`. The
`track_features.py` experiment carries all 12 source identities through hinting
and thickening using corresponding flattened segments plus bounded junction
matching. It rejects ambiguous/colliding matches. At the three confirmed upper
joins, hinting moves the junction by 0.36–0.40px; the exterior wedge remains in the
hinted and grown outlines, but is fully filled in both baseline and final bitmaps.
The metric thus separates movement from loss during bitmap conversion for these
cases. The lower join of `q` is also fully filled; `p` retains only 0.16% of its
lower wedge in the final bitmap.

Joint diagnostics compare baseline, thickened, and constrained candidates. Their
stroke-band penalties are 12/0/0; fully filled junction wedges number 5/10/4. All
retain expected component/counter topology. The constraint repairs six closures
present after thickening, while four source details remain fully filled and one
retains only a sliver of white area. Positive wedge
area alone establishes neither adequate opening nor acceptable contour shape.
Correspondence is approximate; wider fonts/sizes remain unvalidated. Five tracking
tests cover translation, topology mismatch, partial/complete survival, disappearing
features, and the confirmed `bhd` regressions. Results and the stage comparison are
under `tools/font_probe/out/feature-tracking/`; generator settings remain unchanged.

The `correct_pixels.py` trial starts from the shared thickened bitmap and searches
one/two-pixel removals intersecting each tracked junction wedge. A 25% wedge-retention
target is provisional. It preserves ink/counter topology, rejects new diagonal-only
joins, and protects ink runs repeated over three rows. Ranking prefers fewer edits,
then less removed source ink; no glyph-specific correction coordinates are used.

Seven one-pixel candidates pass: both joins of `b`, first joins of `m`, `n`, `p`,
the lower join of `d`, and upper join of `q`. The second `m` join already meets the
trial target. Upper joins of `h` and `d`, and lower joins of `p` and `q`, remain
unresolved under the guards. Thus removal-only search is insufficient within this
neighborhood and constraint set. Candidates await human review; general quality
and transfer to other fonts/sizes remain open. Four synthetic tests exercise stroke
protection, counter closure, diagonal joins, and safe removal. Artifacts and per-case
rejections are under `tools/font_probe/out/pixel-correction/`.

At the user's request, `compare_join_repairs.py` tests local join thinning alongside
pixel relocation. Both start from the guarded corrections and retain ink/counter
topology. Thinning permits diagonal-only connections and removes up to two pixels;
relocation preserves edge connectivity with one removal and one nearby addition.
Both allow local stroke changes and preserve other junction-retention measurements.

Thinning finds candidates for all four remaining joins: one removal each in `h`
and lower `p`, two each in upper `d` and lower `q`. Each introduces diagonal-only
contact at that join. Relocation supports the corrected `h` and `p` with a nearby
added pixel, preserving edge contact; `d` and `q` remain unresolved within its
one-removal/one-addition bound. These are alternative candidates awaiting human
review, not accepted corrections. Three synthetic tests verify the two connectivity
policies, counter preservation, and a supporting pixel relocation. Images and exact
edits are under `tools/font_probe/out/join-repairs/`.

User review prefers the thinning arm (B). `check_transfer.py` applies its settings
unchanged to uppercase, lowercase, and digits at 14/16/18/20px: 248 cases. The seven
16px seed glyphs reproduce exactly. Corrections change 104 glyphs, removing 177
pixels, with no errors or component/counter-count regressions relative to input.
Of 468 detected source junctions, 27 matches remain uncertain and six matched
junctions remain below the provisional retention target. Source topology differs
in 14px `9` and 18px `6`: both already have an extra white hole before correction.

Broader visual inspection reveals notches in `H` crossbar joins and changes to `X`
that structural guards permit. The seven-glyph preference is therefore insufficient
to accept the rule across the repertoire. Detection needs to distinguish useful
entry details from ordinary stroke intersections before correction. Human review
of the new sheets and transfer to other families remain open. Full results and
before/after sheets are under `tools/font_probe/out/transfer-check/`.

`check_transfer.py --classify` adds a source-boundary filter: sample each arm at
0.02/0.055/0.09em from the corner and require at least 5° turning on one arm.
A 12° trial excluded reviewed joins (about 10–12°); 5° separates them from the
straight `H`/`X` arms (0°). The filtered 248-case run changes 47 glyphs/70 pixels,
preserves all seven 16px seeds exactly, and leaves `H`/`X` unchanged at every size.
It excludes 324 junctions; all eligible matched junctions meet the trial target.
The 27 uncertain matches and two pre-existing extra-hole cases remain reported.
Three tests cover reviewed entries, straight intersections, and rigid transforms.
This calibrated geometric proxy awaits broader family and visual validation;
artifacts are under `tools/font_probe/out/classified-transfer/`.

User review rejects the two-pixel upper-stem cut in `d`; the previous seven-glyph
preference is superseded for this correction. `--protect-stems` detects opposed
parallel straight edges in the pre-union grown outlines: overlapping length at
least 0.18em, width 0.4px–0.2em, and at least 95% filled area. Pixels with at least
50% area inside these strips are protected in both removal searches. This
source-derived rule blocks the rejected `d` cut and analogous lower `q` cut.
Their joins remain unresolved; `b`, `h`, `m`, `n`, and `p` match the prior 16px
results. The 248-case run changes 38 glyphs/51 pixels, with no topology regressions;
11 eligible junctions remain below target. Existing uncertainties and extra holes
remain. Three tests cover solid stems, counter rejection, and the exact rejected
`d` pixels. This conservative strip detector is experimental; it has yet to cover
curved or tapering stems. Artifacts are in `tools/font_probe/out/stem-protected-transfer/`.

User accepts leaving stem protection in place but flags `X`. `diagonal_experiment.py`
compares five fixed variants for `XxKVWY` at 14/16/18/20px (120 cases): current,
no growth at the same phase, no growth at grid origin, automatic hinting without
growth, and unhinted without growth. All have one ink component and zero counters;
the 24 current-arm bitmaps exactly match the previous pipeline outputs.
At 16px, `X` uses 48 black pixels with growth versus 40 without it at the same
hinting and phase. This isolates the extra bulk from growth, while hinting changes
need separate visual judgment. No variant is selected automatically. Size comparisons
and the six-glyph 16px sheet are in `tools/font_probe/out/diagonal-experiment/`.

The user proposes small-offset averaging and expects symmetry in `X`.
`offset_experiment.py` holds native mono-target hinting and outline growth fixed,
comparing mono scan conversion, thresholded coverage, and averaged coverage from
nine symmetric offsets (−0.25/0/+0.25px in each axis). Averaging precedes a single
128 threshold. Five extra horizontal phases are tested on `XxKVWY` at four sizes:
360 cases, all with expected topology. Source `X` is exactly left/right symmetric
in the flattened-outline analysis; top/bottom reflection differs by about 7.64%
of source ink area. The averaged binary results still vary by grid phase. No
symmetry constraint is applied and the average is not adopted. Images and raw
coverage are in `tools/font_probe/out/offset-experiment/`. Direct grid-fitting and
filtering literature is now linked in the methodology reference.

## First user image review

Each page labels its exact case and shows the same generated text in normal and
inverse polarity. Annotation labels use a separate host font. The generated font
samples include ambiguous characters, units, punctuation, and clock text.

- **Waveshare-sized images:** the three fonts at caption size, weight 400,
  thresholds 128/96, followed by Source Sans 3 at 40px, thresholds 128/112.
- **Inkplate-sized images:** the three fonts at caption size, weight 400,
  binary/gray pairs.

Present contact sheets and individual PNGs to the user before board testing. Use
native-pixel views for text and nearest-neighbour enlargements for details. Record
the case label, unclear characters, stroke/counter defects, spacing, and preferences
for each polarity. Revise the settings and regenerate comparisons as needed; user
approval identifies the settings to take to the boards. Screen scale and contrast
limit what these images establish about physical reading comfort.

## Later board verification

After user image approval, load the selected cases onto the boards. The prepared
Waveshare viewer cycles through eight pages; the Inkplate gallery advances through
six SD-backed pages by swipe. Adjust these sets to match the approved comparisons.

The exact configured sets and frame hashes are in each target's
`specimens/session.json`. They identify only the current session; stale files are
excluded from the viewer's generated module.

At 40–50cm, record each page's case label and:

1. Recognition errors or unclear characters, especially `8`, `0`, `h`, and `p`.
2. Readability and comfort for normal and inverse text, with lighting described.
3. Visible rendering defects or residual images after a page transition.

These sets provide an initial comparison. Further selected
sessions cover weights, larger sizes at 80–100cm, and repeat-refresh conditions
where existing observations are insufficient. Device logs establish which frame
was displayed; human observations establish how it reads. Keep product font/role
selection separate from this evaluation.
